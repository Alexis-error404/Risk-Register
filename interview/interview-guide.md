# Interview Guide

### Inherent vs residual risk?
“Inherent risk is exposure before considering the effect of relevant controls. Residual risk is what remains after controls/treatment. I would reassess it based on expected or validated control effectiveness rather than subtracting an arbitrary value.”

### Risk owner vs action owner?
“The risk owner is accountable for the business risk and should have appropriate authority. The action owner completes a remediation task. They can be different people.”

### How do you prioritize?
“I'd consider the approved methodology, severity, business/asset criticality, exposure, threat activity, regulatory obligations, control effectiveness, remediation feasibility, and dependencies—not just the numeric score.”

### Close a risk when remediation says complete?
“No. I'd collect evidence, validate control implementation/effectiveness, reassess residual risk, and follow the formal closure or acceptance process.”

### What if leadership accepts Critical risk?
“I'd ensure the decision is made by the authorized risk owner/acceptance authority and document rationale, residual exposure, review date, and compensating controls. A GRC analyst supports the decision rather than silently accepting business risk.”

### Why third-party risk?
“Organizations inherit exposure through vendors that process data or provide critical services, so GRC has to address business dependencies as well as internal technical weaknesses.”

### Closing statement
“My technical background helps me understand what systems and controls are doing. The GRC skill I'm developing is translating that into evidence, control effectiveness, risk, remediation, ownership, and decisions leadership can act on.”
